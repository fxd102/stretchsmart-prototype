House Design — v3 Rural Hillside Residence · NE Pennsylvania

Three Floors — v3 Iteration

Updated May 2 from your three changes: mudroom now bumps out as a 12'×12' square attached to the west wall (back-entrance vestibule); stairs moved from the central spine to the north wall — basement stair down sits on the western half, loft stair up sits on the eastern half. Central interior is now a clear gallery hall connecting mudroom ↔ bedrooms ↔ open east living, with no stair traffic crossing it.

First Floor

~1,680 sf incl. bumpout · main rectangle 48'×32' + mudroom 12'×12' bumpout west · stairs along N wall
Mudroom bumpout: 12'×12' square attached to the west wall, centered N–S. Driveway entry hits this directly. Houses laundry, gear, boots, bench, hooks. Single door connects bumpout to the main building's central gallery hall. NW corner: ADA bedroom (13'×12') with private accessible bath next door — roll-in shower, grab bars, level path from mudroom door. SW corner: Master suite (13'×10') + walk-in closet (7'×6') + luxury bath (17'×6') with multi-head shower and soaking tub, double vanity, water closet. Eastern strip (17'×28'): open living/dining/kitchen with 2-story east glass wall. Kitchen counter under the loft stair on the N wall, island parallel, dining center, living south end. Stairs along N wall: basement stair (12'×4') on the western half; loft stair (12'×4') on the eastern half. 12' of N wall between them is freed for a window or built-in shelving feature. Central gallery hall: with stairs out of the spine, the central E–W corridor becomes a clean architectural feature — connects mudroom door to open east living, with bedrooms north and south.

Basement (Walkout)

east wall = full-width walkout · SE office (fireplace S wall) · NE guest · W half = mech / pantry / bath
Stair landing: arrives in the basement's NW area (under the first-floor stair on the western half of the N wall). Lands "behind" the guest room (i.e., on the dividing wall west of it). NE corner: Guest bedroom (18'×10') with east window/door for daylight, closet built-in. Slightly shorter N–S than v2 because the stair landing now occupies the basement's north strip. SE corner: Office/library (18'×11') with the fieldstone fireplace built into the south wall, east walkout for daylight, acoustic-treated walls for video calls, built-in bookshelves on E + W walls, window seat east. Walkout zone between guest and office: 18'×7' family/media space with patio doors east. Western half: Mechanical (NW corner, 6'×15'), pantry (extending east of mech, ~28'×7' total walk-in), full bath (13'×7'), storage/flex/workshop strip along the south wall. Mudroom above is on slab — no basement under the bumpout. Foundation steps in at the main rectangle's west wall. Earth-bermed on W/N/S sides, walkout grade on E.

Loft / Mezzanine

~384 sf · directly above east-strip living · full N–S width (32') · 12' deep E–W
Loft sits directly above the eastern living strip, taking the full N–S length of the east wall as you specified. 13' deep (E–W) — slightly wider than v2 to absorb the stair landing strip; eastern 5' still open-to-below so the 2-story east glass reads as a full-height window wall. Stair landing: on the loft's NE side, matching the first-floor stair UP that runs along the eastern half of the N wall. You step off the stair and walk south into the loft — the stair lands you at the cool-end (north, lower ceiling under shed roof) and the loft opens up south toward the high ceiling. Three zones (yoga/movement north, reading nook center against glass, study/meditation south) so the brief's "yoga vs library — TBD" doesn't have to be binary. ~364 sf hosts all three.

Open questions for the next pass

  • ADA bath: I included a private accessible bath next to the NW bedroom. Want it private (drawn) or shared with a hall bath instead?
  • Kitchen flip: Counter on N wall, island parallel. Could swap to S end of the open zone if you want the living near the N wall instead.
  • Master bath/closet swap: Closet 7'×5' adjacent to bedroom, bath along south wall. Could swap their positions.
  • First-floor fireplace gone: Removed per spec (moved to office). Sanity check — the firelight in the main living space is a real architectural gesture, and losing it changes the character of evenings in the living room. Worth keeping a second fireplace upstairs?
  • Stairs: Both clustered in the central spine for short circulation. Alternative: stair up at one end of the house, stair down at the other. More space-efficient but more walking.
  • Loft access: Single straight-run stair drawn. Could be open spiral, switchback, or floating-tread design — affects how dramatic the entry to the loft feels.
