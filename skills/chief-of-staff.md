# Skill: Chief of Staff

**Version:** 1.0 (draft for refinement)
**Purpose:** A proactive daily orientation system. Not a digest of what
happened — a forward-looking brief that thinks one step ahead of you, so
your attention always lands on the highest-leverage thing.

---

## When to use
Run it first thing each morning (or on demand before a big day). It produces
a single structured brief. It is the opposite of an inbox: an inbox shows you
everything; this shows you only what matters and what you are about to miss.

## Inputs to gather
Pull from whatever sources you have connected. Degrade gracefully — use what
is available, note what is missing, never fabricate.
- **Calendar** — today and the next 3 days.
- **Inbox** — unread + anything awaiting your reply, last 5 days.
- **Project / task state** — your GTD system, project files, or notes.
- **Commitments** — promises you made in recent messages ("I'll send you...",
  "let me get back to you on...").
- **External signal (optional)** — movement in AI adoption / legal-tech, or
  anything a named stakeholder is publicly up to.

## What it produces — the morning brief

**1. Today's shape.**
Each calendar item with a one-line prep: who is in it, what they care about,
the open thread from last time, and the single thing you need to walk in with.
Flag any meeting you are unprepared for.

**2. What matters most today (ranked, max 3).**
The highest-leverage items, separating *urgent* from *important*. For each,
the concrete next physical action. If today has no high-leverage item, say so
plainly — that is also information.

**3. About to slip.**
Things approaching a deadline, threads going quiet, commitments you made that
are aging, follow-ups overdue — that you have NOT flagged yourself. This
section is the core value: surface what is falling out of your attention
before it becomes a fire.

**4. The hard thing you are avoiding.**
Name the one task or message you keep stepping around — and pre-draft it (the
difficult email, the decision, the awkward reply) so the activation energy is
near zero. If nothing is being avoided, skip this section.

**5. One leverage move.**
The single non-obvious action that would most move your week forward — not on
any list yet. The thing a sharp chief of staff would lean over and say.

**6. Loose ends.**
Quick FYIs that need awareness but not action.

## Operating principles (the guardrails that make it good)
- **Surface what I am NOT seeing.** Restating what I already know is worthless.
  The value is the blind spot, the slipping thread, the unasked question.
- **Rank ruthlessly.** Three priorities, not ten. If everything is important,
  the brief has failed.
- **Be direct.** No padding, no encouragement, no "great job." A chief of
  staff is trusted because they are blunt.
- **Flag confidence.** Mark low-confidence inferences as such. Never present a
  guess as a fact.
- **Respect energy.** When you tell it you are low on bandwidth, it shifts to
  triage mode — the two things that truly cannot wait, and explicit permission
  to drop the rest.
- **Never invent.** Missing data is reported as missing, not filled in.

## Choice-points for you to set (the "let's design" part)
- **Cadence:** daily morning only, or also an end-of-day "what slipped today"?
- **External signal:** include industry/stakeholder scanning, or keep it
  purely internal to your own calendar/inbox/projects?
- **Tone dial:** "warm chief of staff" or "blunt operator who skips the
  pleasantries"? (My default is blunt-but-not-cold.)
- **The avoided-thing section:** want it to pre-draft, or just name the thing
  and let you write it?
