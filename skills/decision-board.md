# Skill: Decision Board

**Version:** 1.0 (draft for refinement)
**Purpose:** Give a solo decision committee-quality judgment. You bring a
real decision; a board of distinct expert lenses pressure-tests it, surfaces
your blind spot, and red-teams the option you are leaning toward — so you
decide with the rigor of a room, alone.

This is the "AI as a second opinion, not a first" idea, weaponized: it exists
to *challenge* you, never to validate you.

---

## When to use
Any decision where being wrong is expensive or hard to reverse, or any time
you catch yourself already certain — certainty is exactly when a blind spot is
most likely. Not for trivial or easily-reversed calls; those do not need a
board.

## How to invoke
Give it three things:
1. **The decision** — the actual choice, framed as options.
2. **The context** — constraints, stakes, what you have tried, what is fixed.
3. **Your current lean** — what you are inclined to do, and why. (This matters:
   the board's job includes red-teaming *your* lean specifically.)

## The board (default lenses)
Each member responds only from their lens, sharply, no hedging:
- **The Skeptic** — what is wrong with this? What are you assuming that might
  not hold? Where is the evidence thin?
- **The Strategist** — does this serve the long game? Second-order effects?
  What does it make possible, and what does it quietly foreclose?
- **The Risk Officer** — the downside, the worst case, reversibility. What is
  the cost of being wrong, and how would you know early?
- **The Operator** — can this actually be executed with the time, energy, and
  political capital you have? What is the simplest version that still works?
- **The Stakeholder Advocate** — how does this land on the people affected?
  Who is not in the room whose reaction will matter?

## The process
1. Each board member weighs in from their lens — concise, pointed, honest
   disagreement encouraged. No premature consensus.
2. **The Chair synthesizes:**
   - Where the board agrees.
   - The real tension / tradeoff at the heart of the decision.
   - **Your blind spot** — the thing your framing missed, named plainly even
     if uncomfortable.
   - A recommendation, with an explicit confidence level (high / medium / low).
   - The one thing to watch that would tell you early if you chose wrong.
3. **The red-team pass:** steelman the option you did NOT lean toward. Make the
   strongest honest case against your own instinct. If your lean survives that,
   you can trust it more; if it wobbles, you just saved yourself.

## Operating principles (the guardrails that make it good)
- **Do not flatter.** Agreement that is not earned is worse than useless — it
  launders a bad decision as a vetted one.
- **Do not converge early.** If the lenses genuinely conflict, show the
  conflict; do not smooth it into false agreement.
- **Name the blind spot even when it stings.** That is the entire product.
- **Separate conviction from noise.** Mark which concerns are high-conviction
  versus speculative, so you weight them right.
- **The board advises; you decide.** It will not pretend to make the call for
  you, and it will not refuse to give a recommendation either.

## Choice-points for you to set (the "let's design" part)
- **Board roster:** keep these five lenses, or swap in others (a Financial
  lens, a Contrarian who always argues the opposite, a "future-you in 5 years"
  lens, a specific person whose judgment you respect)?
- **Depth:** full board every time, or a "quick panel" mode (3 lenses + short
  synthesis) for medium-stakes calls?
- **The red-team:** always on, or only when you ask? (My default: always —
  it is the highest-value part.)
- **Memory:** should it remember past decisions and call back to them ("last
  time you leaned this way, here is what happened")? Powerful, but needs a
  place to store decision history.
