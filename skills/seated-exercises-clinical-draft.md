# Seated Exercises — Clinical Schema (v1 draft, pending PT validation)

Each entry now carries four clinical fields derived from your aunt's feedback:
**Form cue** (primary mover) · **Stop condition** (contraindication) · **Regression** (easier version) · **Remediation** (what to build first).

These 6 span the exercise *types* (postural drill, mobility, stretch, breathing, upper + lower body) to prove the format works across the library before the remaining ~55 seated are done.

---

## Scapular Squeeze  _( Upper Back, difficulty 1 )_

*Movement:* Sit tall, squeeze your shoulder blades together as if pinching a pencil between them. Hold 5 seconds, release.

- **Form cue (primary mover):** Drive it from the shoulder blades pulling DOWN and together (retraction + depression), not from shrugging up. Think 'blades toward your back pockets'; chest stays soft, and don't arch the low back to fake range.
- **Stop condition:** Stop if your shoulders rise toward your ears, if you feel pinching in the front of the shoulder, or any neck strain. The work belongs between the shoulder blades, not in the neck or upper traps.
- **Regression:** Reduce the squeeze to a gentle ~30% effort with a brief hold, or do it lying on your back so the floor gives feedback and gravity assists.
- **Remediation:** If the upper traps dominate (shoulders rise), the lower traps / scapular depressors need work first: practice slow scapular depressions (slide the blades down without squeezing) before adding the squeeze.

## Gentle Neck Nod  _( Neck, difficulty 1 )_

*Movement:* Slowly nod your head forward and back, bringing chin toward chest then looking up. Move smoothly through the full range.

- **Form cue (primary mover):** Move only the head on top of the neck — a small 'yes' nod, jaw relaxed, rest of the spine still. Slow and smooth, no momentum.
- **Stop condition:** Stop with any dizziness, sharp pain, or numbness/tingling into the arms, or if looking up reproduces symptoms. This is mobility, not a stretch to push — don't force the end range.
- **Regression:** Shrink to a tiny nod, or do only the chin-toward-chest half and skip looking up (the more provocative direction for many people).
- **Remediation:** If the range feels stiff or guarded, settle deep-neck-flexor control with chin tucks first, then add range.

## Chin Tuck  _( Neck, difficulty 1 )_

*Movement:* Pull your chin straight back, making a double chin. Hold 5 seconds, release.

- **Form cue (primary mover):** Glide the chin straight back (a gentle 'double chin'), head sliding back over the shoulders — a horizontal motion, NOT a downward nod or a head tip. Small range; feel a light lengthening at the back of the neck.
- **Stop condition:** Stop with any pinching at the base of the skull, dizziness, or arm symptoms. Keep it gentle — this is a motor-control drill, not a strength push.
- **Regression:** Do it lying on your back with the head supported, which removes gravity and makes the correct glide easier to find. Smaller range, fewer reps.
- **Remediation:** If you can't separate the glide from a nod, rest two fingers on your chin and let them guide the straight-back motion until the pattern is learned.

## Wrist Flexor Stretch  _( Wrists/Hands, difficulty 1 )_

*Movement:* Extend one arm forward, palm up. Use the other hand to gently pull fingers back toward you.

- **Form cue (primary mover):** Arm out, palm up; gently draw the hand/fingers back with the other hand until you feel a stretch along the underside of the forearm. Keep the elbow straight — the stretch is in the forearm, not jammed into the wrist joint.
- **Stop condition:** Stop with sharp pain or any tingling/numbness into the hand (that's nerve, not stretch) — ease off at once. A gentle forearm pull is the target; pain is not.
- **Regression:** Bend the elbow slightly to lower intensity, or stretch against a table edge for finer control instead of pulling with the other hand.
- **Remediation:** If range is very limited or symptoms come on fast, shorten the hold and go more often — tendon/nerve tissue responds to gentle, brief, frequent exposure, not aggressive holds.

## Diaphragmatic Breathing  _( Breathing, difficulty 1 )_

*Movement:* Place one hand on your chest and one on your belly. Breathe in through your nose so your belly rises while your chest stays still.

- **Form cue (primary mover):** Breathe so the BELLY rises on the inhale while chest and shoulders stay quiet — a hand on the belly should move more than a hand on the chest. Exhale slow and passive. The diaphragm works, not the neck/shoulder accessory muscles.
- **Stop condition:** Slow down or stop if you feel lightheaded or dizzy (a sign of over-breathing) — return to a natural rhythm, then resume gently. Don't force big breaths.
- **Regression:** Do it lying down with knees bent — the easiest position to feel belly breathing — before progressing to seated.
- **Remediation:** If chest/shoulders keep dominating, lie down with a light book on the belly for feedback and consciously soften the neck and shoulders on each breath.

## Seated Hamstring Stretch  _( Hamstrings, difficulty 1 )_

*Movement:* Sit on the edge of your chair, extend one leg straight with heel on the floor. Hinge forward at the hips keeping back flat.

- **Form cue (primary mover):** Hinge forward from the HIPS with a long, flat back — lead with the chest, don't round the spine. Feel it in the back of the thigh, not the low back. Knee soft, not locked.
- **Stop condition:** Stop if you feel it in your low back (you're rounding — reset the flat back) or any sharp pain or nerve-like tingling down the leg. Gentle thigh tension only.
- **Regression:** Keep a bigger knee bend, hinge less far, or rest the leg on a low stool instead of straightening fully. Less range, same flat-back form.
- **Remediation:** If tight hamstrings pull you into rounding, reduce range until you can keep a flat back — protecting the low back matters more than reach. Practice the hip hinge (sit tall, tip the pelvis forward slightly) to build the pattern.
