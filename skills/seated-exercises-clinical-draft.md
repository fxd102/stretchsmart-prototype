# Seated Exercises — Clinical Schema (all 61, draft pending PT review)

Every seated exercise now carries four clinical fields from your aunt's feedback:
**Form cue** (primary mover) · **Stop / caution** · **Regression** (easier) · **Remediation** (build first).

The 10 flagged **HIGH-RISK** (form errors carry real injury risk) are listed first — those are the ones worth your aunt's eyes. The rest are low-stakes and drafted for launch.

---

## Sit-to-Stand 🔺 HIGH-RISK  _(Balance, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit in a chair, stand up without using your hands, then sit back down slowly. 10 reps.

- **Form cue:** Scoot forward, feet under you, hinge the chest forward over the toes, and drive up through the heels — stand tall, then lower back down with CONTROL, don't drop. Hands-free is the goal, not a rule.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp knee or hip pain, or if you feel unsteady or lightheaded. SAFETY: keep a stable chair behind you and, if balance is at all shaky, a hand near a solid surface — this is a genuine fall-risk movement for frail or elderly users.
- **Easier:** Use the hands to push off, use a higher/firmer chair (or a cushion to raise the seat), or fewer reps. A higher seat makes it much easier.
- **Build first:** If standing hands-free isn't safe yet, keep using the arms and a sturdy surface nearby, and build leg strength before removing support.

## Ankle Band Strengthening 🔺 HIGH-RISK  _(Calves/Ankles, diff 3)_

*Movement:* Loop a band around the ball of one foot, anchor the other end to a table leg. Practice dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion, and eversion against the band.

- **Form cue:** Move the foot slowly through dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion, and eversion against the band — controlled reps, full but pain-free range, moving only the ankle.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp pain (especially outer-ankle on inversion), or if the knee/leg swings to help. Ease the band tension if the movement gets jerky.
- **Easier:** Lighter band or less range; start with just the pain-free directions, especially if recovering from a sprain.
- **Build first:** Master the unloaded ankle circles, pumps, and inversion/eversion first; add the band once those are smooth and pain-free.

## Pelvic Floor Elevator 🔺 HIGH-RISK  _(Core, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Imagine your pelvic floor is an elevator. Gently draw it up to the first floor (light squeeze), second floor (more), third floor (max).

- **Form cue:** Gently lift the pelvic floor in graded stages (like a slow elevator up), then fully RELAX back down between reps — a lift-and-let-go. Keep breathing; don't clench the glutes or hold your breath.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop if you can't fully relax between reps, feel pain, or find yourself bearing DOWN instead of lifting. Over-gripping is counterproductive.
- **Easier:** Use just the first 'floor' (a light lift) and focus on the full release, which matters as much as the lift.
- **Build first:** If you're unsure you're finding the right muscles, this is worth checking with a pelvic-health PT — cueing it as a bear-down is a common mistake.

## Seated Spinal Wave 🔺 HIGH-RISK  _(Full Body, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Sit at the edge of your chair. Round your lower back first, then mid-back, then upper back into a full slouch.

- **Form cue:** Roll into a slouch one segment at a time — low back, then mid, then upper — a smooth wave of flexion, then reverse to stack back up tall. Controlled, not a collapse.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp back pain or any tingling down the legs. If forward-rounding is your provocative direction, keep it small or skip it.
- **Easier:** Move through a smaller range, or just alternate a gentle slouch and sit-tall without the segmental detail.
- **Build first:** If segmenting is hard, practice a simple pelvic tilt (rocking the pelvis) to find low-back movement first.

## Sciatic Nerve Floss 🔺 HIGH-RISK  _(Hamstrings, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Sit tall. Extend one leg while looking up, then bend the knee while tucking your chin.

- **Form cue:** This is a nerve GLIDE, not a stretch: as the knee straightens look UP, as it bends tuck the chin — the two ends move in opposition, smooth and rhythmic. You should feel gentle tension that comes and goes, never a hard stretch you hold.
- **Stop / caution:** Nerve tissue must not be forced. Stop with any sharp, shooting, or burning pain or increasing numbness/tingling down the leg — that means too much, not 'good stretch.' Keep it gentle and brief; never hold at the tension end.
- **Easier:** Reduce the range at both ends (less knee straightening, smaller head movement) and do fewer, slower reps. Small and gentle is the whole point of flossing.
- **Build first:** If even a small glide provokes shooting leg symptoms, stop and flag it — that pattern wants a clinician's eyes before continuing, not more reps.

## Seated Figure Four 🔺 HIGH-RISK  _(Hips, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit tall, cross one ankle over the opposite knee. Gently lean forward keeping your back straight until you feel a stretch in the crossed hip.

- **Form cue:** Cross the ankle over the opposite knee and hinge forward from the HIPS with a flat back — the stretch belongs in the outer hip/glute of the crossed leg, not the knee or low back. Keep the crossed knee relaxed; don't force it down.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with any KNEE pain (it should be felt in the hip), sharp groin pain, or low-back pain from rounding. IMPORTANT: after a hip replacement this crossed-leg position can violate hip precautions — skip it unless a surgeon or PT has cleared this range.
- **Easier:** Don't cross fully — rest the ankle lower on the shin, or hold the shin and hug the knee gently toward the opposite shoulder for a milder version. Hinge less far.
- **Build first:** If crossing the leg is limited or pinches in the front of the hip, stay with the gentler knee-hug version and don't force the cross.

## Seated Hip Flexor Stretch 🔺 HIGH-RISK  _(Hips, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit at the edge of your chair, scoot one hip off the side and let that leg drop back and down. You'll feel the hip flexor stretch on the dropped side.

- **Form cue:** Let the leg drop back and down off the chair edge, tucking the pelvis slightly (posterior tilt) so the stretch lands in the FRONT of the hip/thigh, not the low back. Sit tall and stay stable on the seat.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with low-back pinching (tuck the pelvis more), sharp groin pain, or if you feel unstable on the edge of the chair.
- **Easier:** Let the leg drop back only slightly; a small range still reaches the hip flexor, and hold the chair for stability.
- **Build first:** If the low back arches to 'fake' the stretch, add a light core brace and tuck the pelvis so the hip does the work.

## Lateral Neck Flexion with Resistance 🔺 HIGH-RISK  _(Neck, diff 3)_

*Movement:* Place your hand on the side of your head. Push your head into your hand, resisting the motion with your hand so there's no movement.

- **Form cue:** Press the head sideways INTO the hand while the hand fully resists so NOTHING moves — a pure isometric. Build the push gradually and keep the chin neutral, not poking forward.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop immediately with dizziness, sharp/shooting pain, or any tingling/numbness into the arm. Keep the force moderate — never a maximal strain on the neck. Skip it entirely if you have current neck pain or nerve symptoms.
- **Easier:** Use light pressure and short holds; the goal is control, not strength.
- **Build first:** Master the gentle Chin Tuck and the forehead Isometric first — those build the base control before adding side-load.

## Neck Lateral Flexion with Overpressure 🔺 HIGH-RISK  _(Neck, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Tilt your head to one side. Place the same-side hand on your head and add very gentle additional pressure.

- **Form cue:** Tilt the head to the side, then let the hand add only VERY gentle extra pressure — a light assist, never a pull. The stretch should feel mild along the opposite side of the neck.
- **Stop / caution:** Overpressure on the neck is the higher-risk part — stop the instant you feel pinching, sharp pain, dizziness, or any tingling into the arm. When in doubt, remove the hand.
- **Easier:** Drop the overpressure entirely and do a plain active tilt (like Neck Side Tilt) — most of the benefit, far less risk.
- **Build first:** If your active tilt range is already limited or symptomatic, stay with the gentle active version and skip overpressure until a clinician clears it.

## Neck Flexion Stretch 🔺 HIGH-RISK  _(Neck, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Drop your chin toward your chest. Place both hands behind your head and let the weight of your hands gently deepen the stretch.

- **Form cue:** Drop the chin toward the chest and let ONLY the weight of the hands (not a pull) gently deepen it — the stretch is along the back of the neck, and it stays mild.
- **Stop / caution:** Do NOT pull the head down — just rest the hands. Stop with tingling into the arms, sharp pain, or dizziness. Loaded neck flexion is provocative for some, so go easy.
- **Easier:** Take the hands away and just drop the chin under its own weight — plenty for most people.
- **Build first:** If it pulls or tingles, skip the hand weight and work gentle active neck nods first.

## Diaphragmatic Breathing  _(Breathing, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Place one hand on your chest and one on your belly. Breathe in through your nose so your belly rises while your chest stays still.

- **Form cue:** Breathe so the BELLY rises on the inhale while chest and shoulders stay quiet — a hand on the belly should move more than a hand on the chest. Exhale slow and passive. The diaphragm works, not the neck/shoulder accessory muscles.
- **Stop / caution:** Slow down or stop if you feel lightheaded or dizzy (a sign of over-breathing) — return to a natural rhythm, then resume gently. Don't force big breaths.
- **Easier:** Do it lying down with knees bent — the easiest position to feel belly breathing — before progressing to seated.
- **Build first:** If chest/shoulders keep dominating, lie down with a light book on the belly for feedback and consciously soften the neck and shoulders on each breath.

## Box Breathing  _(Breathing, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Breathe in for 4 counts, hold for 4 counts, breathe out for 4 counts, hold for 4 counts. Repeat for 8 cycles.

- **Form cue:** Even 4-count in, hold, out, hold — smooth and relaxed, shoulders quiet, belly leading the inhale. Don't force the volume.
- **Stop / caution:** Ease off with lightheadedness or air hunger; return to natural breathing, then resume gentler. Skip long breath-holds if you have significant heart or lung conditions unless cleared.
- **Easier:** Shorten the counts (3-3-3-3, or drop the holds) until it's comfortable.
- **Build first:** Build with simple diaphragmatic breathing before adding holds.

## 4-7-8 Breathing  _(Breathing, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Breathe in for 4 counts, hold for 7 counts, breathe out for 8 counts. Repeat 4-6 times.

- **Form cue:** In for 4, hold for 7, long slow out for 8 — the long exhale is the point. Keep it relaxed; the counts can be quick if 7-8 seconds feels long.
- **Stop / caution:** It's common to feel lightheaded at first — if so, do fewer rounds and shorten the hold. Don't do it standing or driving. Ease the hold if you have heart or lung concerns.
- **Easier:** Scale the counts down (keep the exhale the longest phase).
- **Build first:** Master a simple long-exhale breath first, then add the hold.

## Pursed Lip Breathing  _(Breathing, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Breathe in through your nose for 2 counts. Purse your lips like you're blowing out a candle and exhale for 4 counts.

- **Form cue:** Inhale gently through the nose, exhale slowly through pursed lips (like cooling soup) about twice as long as the inhale — relaxed, the exhale passive and long.
- **Stop / caution:** Very safe — just stop with lightheadedness and return to normal. Especially useful for shortness of breath.
- **Easier:** Shorten the counts; keep the exhale longer than the inhale.
- **Build first:** None needed; this is often the easiest breathing pattern to start with.

## Rib Expansion Breathing  _(Breathing, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Place hands on the sides of your ribcage. Breathe in and try to push your ribs outward into your hands.

- **Form cue:** Hands on the side ribs; breathe so the ribs widen INTO the hands (sideways expansion) rather than the shoulders rising. Feel the ribcage go wide.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with lightheadedness. No force — it's about direction of breath, not size.
- **Easier:** Do it lying down, or with lighter breaths, to feel the rib movement first.
- **Build first:** If the shoulders keep rising, soften them and think 'wide,' not 'big.'

## Sighing Exhale  _(Breathing, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Take a deep breath in through your nose, then exhale through your mouth with an audible sigh. Repeat 5 times.

- **Form cue:** Full easy breath in through the nose, then a complete audible sigh out through the mouth — let the exhale fully empty and the body soften with it.
- **Stop / caution:** Very safe; stop only with lightheadedness, and do fewer gentler rounds if so.
- **Easier:** Smaller breaths; the sigh-out matters more than the size of the in-breath.
- **Build first:** None needed.

## Alternate Nostril Breathing  _(Breathing, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Use your thumb to close one nostril, inhale through the other. Switch and exhale.

- **Form cue:** Close one nostril, inhale through the open side, switch, exhale through the other — slow, even, unforced, shoulders and face relaxed.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with lightheadedness, or if a nostril is too congested to breathe comfortably — don't strain through a blocked side.
- **Easier:** Skip any breath-holds, shorten the counts, or just do a few rounds.
- **Build first:** If congested, do a simpler breathing pattern that day.

## Extended Exhale Breathing  _(Breathing, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Breathe in for 3 counts, breathe out for 6 counts. The extended exhale activates your parasympathetic nervous system.

- **Form cue:** In for 3, out for 6 — the exhale about double the inhale, which is what down-shifts the nervous system. Smooth and belly-led.
- **Stop / caution:** Ease off with any lightheadedness and return to natural breathing.
- **Easier:** Any in:out ratio where the exhale is longer works — scale it to comfort.
- **Build first:** None; this is a gentle, high-value pattern.

## Ankle Circles  _(Calves/Ankles, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Lift one foot off the floor and draw big circles with your toes. 10 circles clockwise, 10 counterclockwise, then switch feet.

- **Form cue:** Lift the foot and draw big slow circles from the ankle, through full range both directions — movement at the ankle, not the whole leg.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp ankle pain or cramping; a little calf cramp means ease the range.
- **Easier:** Smaller circles, fewer reps, or keep the heel resting and just move the forefoot.
- **Build first:** If the ankle is stiff, warm up with gentle pumps first.

## Ankle Alphabet  _(Calves/Ankles, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Lift one foot and trace the alphabet in the air with your big toe. Switch feet and repeat.

- **Form cue:** Trace the letters with the big toe, moving from the ankle through its full range — slow and deliberate, letting the ankle explore all directions.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp pain or cramping; work within a comfortable range.
- **Easier:** Trace fewer letters or make them smaller.
- **Build first:** If it cramps, point-and-flex a few times first and stay hydrated.

## Toe Yoga  _(Calves/Ankles, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Lift your big toe while keeping the other four down, then switch — lift the four toes and press the big toe down. Alternate 10 times each foot.

- **Form cue:** Isolate the toes — lift the big toe while the other four stay down, then reverse. Keep the foot flat and calm; it's about control, not force, and it feels awkward at first.
- **Stop / caution:** Very low risk; just stop and shake it out if the foot cramps.
- **Easier:** Work toward the movement even if the toes barely separate at first — the skill builds with practice.
- **Build first:** If a cramp hits, massage the arch and do fewer reps.

## Toe Spread  _(Calves/Ankles, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Spread all your toes as wide as possible, hold 5 seconds, then relax. Repeat 10 times.

- **Form cue:** Spread all the toes as wide as comfortable, hold, then relax fully. Keep the foot flat and the effort in the toes.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop if the foot cramps; ease off and shake out.
- **Easier:** Assist by hand at first (spread the toes with your fingers) to learn the motion.
- **Build first:** Frequent short sets beat long holds for toe control.

## Seated Ankle Pumps  _(Calves/Ankles, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit with feet flat on the floor. Alternate pointing toes down then pulling toes up, pumping the ankle joint.

- **Form cue:** Alternate pointing the toes down and pulling them up, pumping through the full ankle range — smooth and rhythmic, feet near the floor.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp pain or cramping. (Great for circulation and very low risk.)
- **Easier:** Smaller, slower pumps; even a little movement aids circulation.
- **Build first:** If the calf cramps on the point, favor the pull-up (dorsiflexion) direction.

## Ankle Inversion/Eversion  _(Calves/Ankles, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Sit with feet flat. Roll your feet to the outside edges (inversion), hold 3 seconds, then roll to inside edges (eversion), hold 3 seconds.

- **Form cue:** Roll the soles gently toward each other (inversion) then apart (eversion), moving from the ankle with the heel fairly still — controlled and small, since this is the direction ankles sprain.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp pain, especially on the OUTER ankle (inversion is the classic sprain direction). Gentle range only.
- **Easier:** Very small movements; if recovering from a sprain, work the pain-free direction only.
- **Build first:** Build tolerance with pain-free pumps and circles before loading inversion/eversion.

## Chest Squeeze Isometric  _(Chest, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Press palms together at chest height and squeeze as hard as you can. Hold 10 seconds, release.

- **Form cue:** Press the palms together hard at chest height and hold, feeling the chest work — keep the shoulders DOWN (not shrugging) and KEEP BREATHING through the hold.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with shoulder or wrist pain, or if the shoulders creep up to the ears. Don't hold the breath (it can spike blood pressure).
- **Easier:** Lighter squeeze, shorter hold, hands lower.
- **Build first:** If the shoulders shrug, cue them down first; if the wrists complain, press with loose fists instead of flat palms.

## Seated Core Brace  _(Core, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit tall, tighten your core as if bracing for a light punch to the stomach. Hold 10 seconds while breathing normally.

- **Form cue:** Firm the whole trunk to a moderate 30-40% brace — as if expecting a light poke — and KEEP BREATHING. Don't suck the belly in or hold your breath.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop if you hold your breath and get lightheaded, or with any sharp back pain. It's a gentle brace, not a max clench.
- **Easier:** Lighter brace, shorter hold; even a soft co-contraction counts.
- **Build first:** If you can't brace and breathe at once, practice diaphragmatic breathing first, then add a light brace on top.

## Seated Marching  _(Core, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit tall with core braced. Slowly lift one knee, lower it, then lift the other.

- **Form cue:** Keep the core lightly braced and the spine tall, and lift each knee without rocking the pelvis or leaning back — the trunk stays quiet while the legs move.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with low-back pain, or if you have to swing or lean to lift the knee. Keep it controlled.
- **Easier:** Lift the knee less high, or slide the heel forward instead of lifting.
- **Build first:** If the pelvis rocks, master the Seated Core Brace first so the trunk can stay still.

## Desk Reset Sequence  _(Full Body, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Chin tuck (10 sec), shoulder shrug release (5 reps), wrist circles (10 each way), seated twist (10 sec each side), ankle circles (10 each way). A 2-minute desk break.

- **Form cue:** Move through each mini-exercise gently and in order — it's a mobility reset, not a workout. Follow each component's own gentle, controlled form.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop any single component that provokes pain, dizziness, or tingling — skip it and continue with the rest.
- **Easier:** Do fewer of the components, or fewer reps of each.
- **Build first:** See each individual exercise's cues if any part feels off.

## Progressive Muscle Relaxation  _(Full Body, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Tense each muscle group for 5 seconds then release: feet, calves, thighs, glutes, core, hands, arms, shoulders, face. Focus on the contrast between tension and relaxation.

- **Form cue:** Tense each group to a MODERATE (not maximal) level for about 5 seconds, then fully release and notice the contrast. Keep breathing; never clench hard enough to cramp or strain a joint.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop tensing any area that's injured or wants to cramp, and don't hold the breath.
- **Easier:** Use lighter tension, or skip any sore/injured group and just relax it.
- **Build first:** If a group cramps (calves and feet are common), use gentler tension there.

## Jaw Release  _(Full Body, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Let your jaw hang open. Place your tongue on the roof of your mouth.

- **Form cue:** Let the jaw hang loose and rest the tongue lightly on the roof of the mouth behind the teeth — a passive release into a neutral, relaxed position.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with any jaw-joint (TMJ) pain or clicking that hurts. It should feel like a release, not a stretch.
- **Easier:** Just soften the jaw without any specific tongue placement.
- **Build first:** If the jaw is painful or clicky (TMJ issues), keep it very gentle and see a clinician if it persists.

## Eye Circles  _(Full Body, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Without moving your head, trace a large circle with your eyes — look up, right, down, left. 5 circles each direction.

- **Form cue:** Keep the head still and move ONLY the eyes in slow full circles — up, out, down, in — smooth tracking, without straining to the very corners.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with eye strain, headache, or dizziness — reduce the range and slow down; rest if the eyes feel tired.
- **Easier:** Smaller circles, fewer reps, blink between sets.
- **Build first:** If it triggers dizziness, do fewer and slower, and look at a far point to reset.

## Seated Hamstring Stretch  _(Hamstrings, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit on the edge of your chair, extend one leg straight with heel on the floor. Hinge forward at the hips keeping back flat.

- **Form cue:** Hinge forward from the HIPS with a long, flat back — lead with the chest, don't round the spine. Feel it in the back of the thigh, not the low back. Knee soft, not locked.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop if you feel it in your low back (you're rounding — reset the flat back) or any sharp pain or nerve-like tingling down the leg. Gentle thigh tension only.
- **Easier:** Keep a bigger knee bend, hinge less far, or rest the leg on a low stool instead of straightening fully. Less range, same flat-back form.
- **Build first:** If tight hamstrings pull you into rounding, reduce range until you can keep a flat back — protecting the low back matters more than reach. Practice the hip hinge (sit tall, tip the pelvis forward slightly) to build the pattern.

## Seated Lower Back Flexion  _(Lower Back, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit at the edge of your chair. Slowly round forward, letting your hands hang toward the floor between your knees.

- **Form cue:** Slowly round forward and let the head and arms hang heavy, one segment at a time — a gentle flexion, not a fast flop. Come up slowly, stacking the spine.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp pain or any tingling/numbness down the legs. If flexion is your provocative direction, skip it. Rise slowly to avoid dizziness.
- **Easier:** Round only partway, or rest the hands on the thighs to support some weight on the way down and up.
- **Build first:** If standing up makes you lightheaded, pause halfway. If flexion aggravates the back, favor extension-biased moves instead.

## Gentle Neck Nod  _(Neck, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Slowly nod your head forward and back, bringing chin toward chest then looking up. Move smoothly through the full range.

- **Form cue:** Move only the head on top of the neck — a small 'yes' nod, jaw relaxed, rest of the spine still. Slow and smooth, no momentum.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with any dizziness, sharp pain, or numbness/tingling into the arms, or if looking up reproduces symptoms. This is mobility, not a stretch to push — don't force the end range.
- **Easier:** Shrink to a tiny nod, or do only the chin-toward-chest half and skip looking up (the more provocative direction for many people).
- **Build first:** If the range feels stiff or guarded, settle deep-neck-flexor control with chin tucks first, then add range.

## Neck Side Tilt  _(Neck, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Tilt your right ear toward your right shoulder, hold 5 seconds, then repeat on the left. Keep shoulders down and relaxed.

- **Form cue:** Tip the ear toward the shoulder with the face pointing forward — no rotation — and keep both shoulders down; don't lift the opposite shoulder up to meet the head.
- **Stop / caution:** Ease off with any dizziness, sharp pain, or tingling into the arm. A gentle side-of-neck stretch is the target; don't force the end range.
- **Easier:** Shrink the tilt, or hold the chair with the opposite hand to keep that shoulder anchored down instead of pulling the head over.
- **Build first:** If it feels tight and guarded, warm up with a few gentle neck nods and shoulder rolls first.

## Chin Tuck  _(Neck, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Pull your chin straight back, making a double chin. Hold 5 seconds, release.

- **Form cue:** Glide the chin straight back (a gentle 'double chin'), head sliding back over the shoulders — a horizontal motion, NOT a downward nod or a head tip. Small range; feel a light lengthening at the back of the neck.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with any pinching at the base of the skull, dizziness, or arm symptoms. Keep it gentle — this is a motor-control drill, not a strength push.
- **Easier:** Do it lying on your back with the head supported, which removes gravity and makes the correct glide easier to find. Smaller range, fewer reps.
- **Build first:** If you can't separate the glide from a nod, rest two fingers on your chin and let them guide the straight-back motion until the pattern is learned.

## Upper Trap Stretch  _(Neck, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Hold the bottom of your chair with one hand, tilt your head away from that side. You should feel a stretch from neck to shoulder.

- **Form cue:** Anchor the opposite shoulder down (hold the chair) and tilt the head away, letting the stretch come from the lengthening side — not from yanking with the free hand.
- **Stop / caution:** A gentle pull along the neck-to-shoulder line only; stop with sharp pain, dizziness, or arm tingling.
- **Easier:** Skip the hand-assist and just tilt the head; the anchored shoulder alone gives a stretch.
- **Build first:** If one side is much tighter, spend more time there but never push harder — frequency beats force.

## Levator Scapulae Stretch  _(Neck, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Turn your head 45 degrees to one side, then look down toward your armpit. Use your hand to gently add pressure.

- **Form cue:** Rotate about 45 degrees, drop the nose toward the armpit, and let the hand add only feather-light guidance — the stretch is in the back corner of the neck, not from cranking the head down.
- **Stop / caution:** Very gentle only. Stop with pinching, dizziness, or tingling into the arm or hand. If the overpressure hurts, drop the hand entirely.
- **Easier:** Do it with NO hand pressure — the head position alone stretches the levator for most people.
- **Build first:** If the shoulder on that side hikes up, anchor it down (sit on the hand or hold the chair) before adding the stretch.

## Isometric Neck Strengthening  _(Neck, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Place your palm against your forehead and push your head into it without moving. Hold 5 seconds.

- **Form cue:** Push the head into the palm with NO actual movement — it's a static hold. Build the force gradually so the head stays perfectly still; don't jerk into it.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with dizziness, sharp pain, or symptoms into the arms. Keep the effort moderate — this isn't a max-force test.
- **Easier:** Use light pressure (20-30%) and a shorter hold; even gentle isometrics build control.
- **Build first:** If holding still is hard, rest the head against a wall or headrest and press lightly into that first.

## Neck Protraction and Retraction  _(Neck, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Slowly push your head forward (protraction) then pull it back (retraction/chin tuck). Move through the full range 10 times.

- **Form cue:** Glide the head forward, then draw it straight back into a gentle chin tuck — a horizontal slide, smooth and controlled, not a nod or a forceful jam backward.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with dizziness, pinching at the base of the skull, or arm symptoms. Keep the range comfortable; retraction is the useful end.
- **Easier:** Reduce the range and slow it down; even small glides train the pattern.
- **Build first:** If the retraction end pinches, stick with plain Chin Tucks until it settles.

## Seated Knee Extension  _(Quads, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit tall, straighten one leg until it's parallel to the floor. Hold 5 seconds, lower slowly.

- **Form cue:** Straighten the knee to about parallel with the floor, squeezing the thigh at the top — slow up, slow down, thigh staying on the seat.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp knee pain (a mild working effort in the thigh is fine). Ease off if the knee clicks painfully.
- **Easier:** Straighten only partway, or do fewer reps; a partial extension still works the quad.
- **Build first:** If the knee aches at full lock, stop just short of straight and hold there.

## Shoulder Shrug Release  _(Shoulders, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Shrug your shoulders up to your ears, hold 5 seconds, then drop them completely. Repeat 5 times.

- **Form cue:** Shrug straight up toward the ears, then let them DROP and fully release — the value is in the letting-go, not the lift. Keep breathing.
- **Stop / caution:** A gentle move; stop with any neck pinch or sharp shoulder pain.
- **Easier:** Smaller shrug, or do one shoulder at a time.
- **Build first:** If the shoulders live up near the ears, pair this with scapular depressions to learn the 'down' position.

## Scapular Squeeze  _(Upper Back, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit tall, squeeze your shoulder blades together as if pinching a pencil between them. Hold 5 seconds, release.

- **Form cue:** Drive it from the shoulder blades pulling DOWN and together (retraction + depression), not from shrugging up. Think 'blades toward your back pockets'; chest stays soft, and don't arch the low back to fake range.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop if your shoulders rise toward your ears, if you feel pinching in the front of the shoulder, or any neck strain. The work belongs between the shoulder blades, not in the neck or upper traps.
- **Easier:** Reduce the squeeze to a gentle ~30% effort with a brief hold, or do it lying on your back so the floor gives feedback and gravity assists.
- **Build first:** If the upper traps dominate (shoulders rise), the lower traps / scapular depressors need work first: practice slow scapular depressions (slide the blades down without squeezing) before adding the squeeze.

## Thoracic Extension  _(Upper Back, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit on a chair, clasp hands behind your head, and gently arch your upper back over the chair back. Hold 5 seconds, return.

- **Form cue:** Arch the UPPER back over the chair edge to open the chest — the movement is in the mid-back, not the low back. Support the head with the hands; don't crank the neck.
- **Stop / caution:** Keep it gentle and in the mid-back. Stop with low-back pinching (you're arching too low), sharp pain, or dizziness.
- **Easier:** Reduce the arch, or do it without clasping the head — just open the arms and lift the chest.
- **Build first:** If the low back takes over, brace the core lightly and keep the ribs down so the motion stays in the thoracic spine.

## Thoracic Rotation  _(Upper Back, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit tall with arms crossed over your chest. Rotate your torso to the right, hold 5 seconds, then to the left.

- **Form cue:** Rotate from the mid-back with arms crossed, keeping the hips and pelvis facing forward — turn the ribcage, not the whole body. Sit tall throughout.
- **Stop / caution:** Gentle rotation only; stop with sharp back pain or anything radiating.
- **Easier:** Reduce the turn; small controlled range beats forcing depth.
- **Build first:** If the hips swivel, plant the feet and squeeze the seat to keep the base still, isolating the mid-back.

## Seated Twist  _(Upper Back, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Sit tall, place your right hand on your left knee and twist your torso to the left. Hold 15 seconds, repeat on the other side.

- **Form cue:** Sit tall and rotate the ribcage, using the hand on the knee only as a light gauge — not a lever to wrench yourself around. Grow taller as you turn; don't collapse.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp or pinching back pain, or anything shooting down a leg. This is mobility, not a max stretch.
- **Easier:** Use less rotation and don't pull on the knee; a gentle turn is enough.
- **Build first:** Keep the spine long — rotation on a tall spine is safer than cranking on a slumped one.

## Seated Shoulder Blade Pinch  _(Upper Back, diff 1)_

*Movement:* While seated at your desk, pull your shoulder blades down and together, as if putting them in your back pockets. Hold 5 seconds, release.

- **Form cue:** Pull the shoulder blades DOWN and together — 'into the back pockets' — not shrugging up. Chest stays soft; the work is between the blades.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop if the shoulders rise toward the ears, or with front-of-shoulder pinching or neck strain.
- **Easier:** A lighter squeeze and a briefer hold.
- **Build first:** If the upper traps dominate, practice scapular depression (blades down without squeezing) first.

## Seated Scapular Depression  _(Upper Back, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Place hands on the seat of your chair. Push down to lift your body slightly, focusing on pulling shoulder blades down.

- **Form cue:** Press the hands into the seat to lightly unweight, driving the shoulder blades DOWN — long neck, ears away from shoulders. A small, controlled lift.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with wrist pain, front-of-shoulder pinching, or if you can't keep the shoulders down. Don't hang on locked, shrugged shoulders.
- **Easier:** Skip the lift — just press lightly and focus on drawing the blades down without leaving the seat.
- **Build first:** Build wrist tolerance and the scapular-down pattern before putting body weight through the arms.

## Wrist Circles  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Extend arms forward, make fists, and circle your wrists.

- **Form cue:** Make loose fists and circle the wrists through full range both ways — movement at the wrist, forearms still, slow and smooth.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp wrist pain or painful clicking; mild stiffness easing is fine.
- **Easier:** Smaller circles, or unclenched hands.
- **Build first:** If stiff, shake the hands out to warm them first.

## Prayer Stretch  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Press palms together in a prayer position at chest height. Slowly lower hands while keeping palms pressed together until you feel a wrist stretch.

- **Form cue:** Palms pressed flat together at chest height, lower the hands until a gentle stretch appears on the underside of the wrists/forearms — keep the palms together and the elbows lifted.
- **Stop / caution:** A gentle stretch only; stop with sharp pain or tingling into the hands.
- **Easier:** Lower the hands less far, or ease the palm pressure.
- **Build first:** If very tight, hold higher and build range gradually with frequent short holds.

## Reverse Prayer Stretch  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Press the backs of your hands together in front of your chest with fingers pointing down. Hold 15 seconds.

- **Form cue:** Press the backs of the hands together, fingers pointing down, to stretch the top of the wrists/forearms. Keep it gentle and symmetrical.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp pain or tingling; a mild top-of-forearm stretch is the goal.
- **Easier:** Reduce the press, or do one wrist at a time with the other hand assisting gently.
- **Build first:** Build tolerance slowly — this position is intense for tight wrists.

## Finger Spread  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Spread all fingers wide apart, hold 5 seconds, then make a tight fist, hold 5 seconds. Alternate 8 times.

- **Form cue:** Spread the fingers as wide as comfortable, then close to a firm (not white-knuckle) fist — smooth open and close.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with joint pain; mild effort is fine. Very low risk.
- **Easier:** Smaller range, fewer reps.
- **Build first:** If arthritic joints ache, warm the hands first and reduce the squeeze.

## Wrist Flexor Stretch  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Extend one arm forward, palm up. Use the other hand to gently pull fingers back toward you.

- **Form cue:** Arm out, palm up; gently draw the hand/fingers back with the other hand until you feel a stretch along the underside of the forearm. Keep the elbow straight — the stretch is in the forearm, not jammed into the wrist joint.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp pain or any tingling/numbness into the hand (that's nerve, not stretch) — ease off at once. A gentle forearm pull is the target; pain is not.
- **Easier:** Bend the elbow slightly to lower intensity, or stretch against a table edge for finer control instead of pulling with the other hand.
- **Build first:** If range is very limited or symptoms come on fast, shorten the hold and go more often — tendon/nerve tissue responds to gentle, brief, frequent exposure, not aggressive holds.

## Wrist Extensor Stretch  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Extend one arm forward, palm down. Use the other hand to gently push the wrist down and back.

- **Form cue:** Arm forward palm DOWN, gently guide the hand down and back with the other hand until a stretch appears on TOP of the forearm — elbow straight, stretch in the forearm, not jammed into the wrist joint.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp pain or tingling into the hand — ease off at once. Gentle pull only.
- **Easier:** Bend the elbow slightly, or ease the pressure.
- **Build first:** Short frequent holds beat long aggressive ones for forearm tissue.

## Rubber Band Finger Extension  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Place a rubber band around all five fingertips. Spread your fingers apart against the resistance.

- **Form cue:** Open the fingers against the band's resistance slowly, then CONTROL the return — don't let it snap shut. It trains the finger extensors.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with joint pain; keep the effort moderate.
- **Easier:** Use a lighter/looser band, or fewer reps.
- **Build first:** Start with plain Finger Spread (no band) if the resistance is too much.

## Grip Squeeze  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Squeeze a stress ball or rolled-up towel as hard as you can for 5 seconds, then release. 10 reps each hand.

- **Form cue:** Squeeze the ball or towel firmly for the hold, then fully release — steady effort, not a painful crush.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with joint pain (especially arthritic finger/thumb joints); mild forearm fatigue is fine.
- **Easier:** Softer object, lighter squeeze, fewer reps.
- **Build first:** If arthritic, use a softer ball and less force; warmth first helps.

## Tendon Glide Sequence  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Move through five hand positions: straight fingers, hook fist, full fist, tabletop (fingers bent at knuckles), straight fist. Hold each 5 seconds.

- **Form cue:** Move slowly through the five hand positions, pausing at each — these glide the finger tendons through their range. Smooth and controlled.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp pain or a finger catching/locking. Mild stretch or pull is fine.
- **Easier:** Do fewer positions, or hold each briefly; even partial glides help.
- **Build first:** This is common in hand therapy for stiffness — if a finger catches or locks, flag it to a clinician.

## Thumb Stretch and Opposition  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Touch your thumb to each fingertip in sequence, then stretch your thumb away from your hand. 8 opposition cycles plus a 10-second stretch each hand.

- **Form cue:** Touch the thumb to each fingertip in turn (opposition), then gently stretch the thumb away from the palm — precise and controlled, the stretch mild.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with pain at the base of the thumb (a common arthritic spot); ease the stretch.
- **Easier:** Do opposition only and skip the stretch, or reduce the stretch range.
- **Build first:** If the thumb base is arthritic or painful, keep it gentle and brief; warmth first helps.

## Finger Abduction with Band  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 3)_

*Movement:* Wrap a small rubber band around all five fingers at the middle knuckle. Spread fingers apart against the resistance.

- **Form cue:** Spread the fingers apart against the band at the middle knuckles, then control the return — it trains the small hand muscles. Slow and deliberate.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with joint pain; moderate effort only.
- **Easier:** Looser band, fewer reps, or plain finger spreads first.
- **Build first:** Build with unresisted Finger Spread before adding the band.

## Forearm Pronation/Supination  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 1)_

*Movement:* Rest forearm on your desk, hand hanging off the edge. Rotate palm down then palm up slowly.

- **Form cue:** Forearm resting on the desk, hand off the edge; rotate slowly palm-down then palm-up, keeping the elbow and forearm still so the motion is at the forearm, not the shoulder.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp wrist or elbow pain. Smooth, controlled rotation only.
- **Easier:** Smaller range, or hold a light object for feedback (not weight).
- **Build first:** If the elbow lifts to cheat, pin it to the desk or your side.

## Wrist CARs  _(Wrists/Hands, diff 2)_

*Movement:* Make a fist. Slowly draw the biggest circle you can with your wrist, moving through full range including flexion, extension, and side-to-side.

- **Form cue:** Make a fist and draw the biggest slow circle the wrist can, deliberately reaching full flexion, extension, and both sides — controlled rotation, forearm still. Quality and range over speed.
- **Stop / caution:** Stop with sharp pain or painful clicking; work to the edge of comfortable range, not into pain.
- **Easier:** Smaller circles; build the range over time.
- **Build first:** Warm up with plain wrist circles before pushing full range.
