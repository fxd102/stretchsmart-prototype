# Grey Wolf — Making It Livable After 5 Years Sitting

Work top to bottom. **Do Section 1 before you sleep in it or light anything.**
⚠️ = life-safety, don't skip or defer.

---

## 1. Safety first — before anything else

- [ ] ⚠️ **LP leak test.** Five years dries out seals. Soapy water on every connection with the tank valve open, watch for bubbles — or have an RV shop pressure-test it. Do this BEFORE lighting any appliance.
- [ ] ⚠️ **CO detector — check the expiry date stamp.** They expire in ~5–7 years. If it's expired or the date is unreadable, replace it. This kills people in campers; it is the single most important item on this list.
- [ ] ⚠️ **LP/propane detector** — same, they expire. Replace if due.
- [ ] ⚠️ **Smoke detector** — new battery, test it.
- [ ] ⚠️ **Fire extinguisher** — check gauge is in the green and the date; replace if expired.
- [ ] Open everything up and air it out for a day before you commit to sleeping in it.

## 2. Pests and water damage (the two things that ruin stored campers)

- [ ] Inspect for rodents: droppings, nests, chewed wiring. Check under the dinette, in cabinets, behind the fridge, and in the furnace + water heater compartments.
- [ ] Seal entry gaps (steel wool + expanding foam), set traps.
- [ ] **Press-test the floor** for soft spots — especially corners, around the toilet, by the door, under windows. Soft = water got in.
- [ ] Check walls/ceiling for stains, bubbling, delamination.
- [ ] Sniff for mildew. A musty smell means moisture is still active somewhere.

## 3. Roof and seals — do this early, before rain

- [ ] Get on the roof (carefully — check it holds weight first). Inspect ALL sealant: around vents, AC unit, skylight, antenna, and every seam.
- [ ] Look for cracking, lifting, or gaps. Reseal with **Dicor self-leveling lap sealant**.
- [ ] Check window and door seals from outside; reseal as needed.
- [ ] Check the awning fabric for mildew/tears; extend and clean it.

## 4. Fresh water system

- [ ] If it was winterized, flush ALL the RV antifreeze out (run every tap until clear, don't forget the shower + toilet sprayer).
- [ ] **Sanitize the fresh tank and lines** — bleach solution or RV sanitizer, let it sit, then flush thoroughly. Five years stagnant grows biofilm.
- [ ] **Water heater:** drain it, flush the sediment, inspect/replace the anode rod (Suburban only), check for leaks. *Never* fire it while empty.
- [ ] Hook to city water **through the pressure regulator** and check EVERY fixture and under every sink for leaks. Dried seals often weep after long storage.
- [ ] Test the 12V water pump (run it dry-ish only briefly).
- [ ] Toilet: does the bowl hold water? A dried-out flush-ball seal won't — a common 5-year failure, and it lets tank odor into the camper.

## 5. Waste tanks

- [ ] Operate the black and gray dump valves — they seize after sitting. Free them up before you need them.
- [ ] Flush the black tank thoroughly (a tank rinser helps).
- [ ] Confirm the tank level sensors read (often gunked up — not critical, just know they may lie).

## 6. Propane appliances (only after the leak test in §1)

- [ ] Tanks: check the recert date stamp on the collar, refill.
- [ ] Stove/oven — light each burner.
- [ ] Furnace — run it; listen for the blower and confirm it actually heats.
- [ ] Water heater on LP.
- [ ] **Fridge** — test on BOTH propane and electric. Absorption fridges are the classic 5-year casualty (ammonia settles/crystallizes). Give it several hours before judging. If it won't cool on either, that's the expensive one.

## 7. Electrical

- [ ] **Batteries** — test voltage. After 5 years, plan on replacing. Check for corrosion/swelling; clean terminals.
- [ ] Test the converter/charger (is it actually charging the battery when on shore power?).
- [ ] Test every 120V outlet and all 12V lights.
- [ ] **Trip and reset the GFCI** — they fail with age.
- [ ] **Run the air conditioner** — confirm it actually cools, and listen for a rough compressor start. This matters given you're planning to run AC on the shared power.

## 8. Chassis and tires — before it EVER moves

- [ ] ⚠️ **Tires: read the DOT date code** (last 4 digits = week + year of manufacture). **RV tires age out at 5–7 years regardless of tread depth.** Sitting causes flat-spotting and sidewall cracking. Assume replacement.
- [ ] Check pressure against the sidewall max / placard.
- [ ] Inspect sidewalls for cracks, especially where it sat on the sun side.
- [ ] Wheel bearings — repack before any real towing.
- [ ] Test all trailer lights and brakes if it's going anywhere.
- [ ] Look under for frame rust, hanging wires, critter nests.

## 9. Mechanicals and finish

- [ ] Slide-outs (if any): operate fully, lubricate the seals, check for daylight.
- [ ] Stabilizer jacks: operate and grease.
- [ ] Windows and roof vents: open/close each, check screens, test the vent fan.
- [ ] Door locks + keys work.
- [ ] Deep clean, then leave a moisture absorber (DampRid) in it.

## 10. Paper

- [ ] Registration and insurance current, if it'll be towed on a road.

---

**Suggested order:** §1 safety → §2 pests/damage → §3 roof → §4 water → §7 electrical →
§6 propane appliances → §5 tanks → §8 tires (before towing) → §9/§10.

**The three that would actually hurt you:** expired CO detector, an undetected LP leak,
and 5-year-old tires. Everything else is comfort and money.
