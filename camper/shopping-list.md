# Camper Hookup — Shopping List

Two campers (2014 Jayco Jay Flight 32', 30A · Grey Wolf toy hauler ~22', 30A) sharing
one 50A pedestal and one 1,000-gal in-ground tank. Runs: 3' and 25'.

⚠️ = safety-relevant, don't substitute.

---

## Electrical

| # | Item | Spec / why | ~Cost |
|---|------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | **50A-to-two-30A Y splitter** | NEMA 14-50P → dual TT-30R. **Get 10-gauge / heavy duty** — the pedestal's 50A breaker is protecting this cord's wiring. Camco, RVGuard, or Nilight. | $40–70 |
| 2 | **30A extension cord** (if needed) | Only if a camper's stock cord won't reach the splitter. TT-30, 10-gauge, 25–30'. Don't use a household extension cord. ⚠️ | $50–70 |
| 3 | **Surge protector / EMS**, 30A ×2 | Optional but smart — protects against miswired pedestals and surges. Extra worthwhile on a rig that's sat 5 years. Progressive Industries or Camco. | $80–120 ea |

## Sewer

| # | Item | Spec / why | ~Cost |
|---|------|-----------|-------|
| 4 | **RhinoFLEX hose, 20' + 10'** | For the 25' run. Use the FEWEST sections that reach — every joint is a sag/leak point. (The 3' camper likely just needs a short 10'.) | $45–70 |
| 5 | **Sewer hose support, 30'** | Camco Sidewinder 30' **or** Slunky 25'. This is what holds your downhill grade — the single most important accessory for a 25' run. | $40–60 |
| 6 | **Sewer wye (Y) fitting** | Joins both camper hoses into one tank inlet. Bayonet-style. Valterra or Camco. **Install it AT THE TANK**, not near a camper. | $15–25 |
| 7 | **Clear elbow** ← you asked | Camco clear 90° sewer elbow w/ bayonet. Lets you see when the line runs clear so you know it's actually empty. | $12–20 |
| 8 | Spare bayonet seals/gaskets | They dry out and leak. Cheap insurance. | $8 |
| 9 | Disposable gloves + a dedicated bin | Sewer gear never shares storage with anything else. | $15 |

## Fresh water

| # | Item | Spec / why | ~Cost |
|---|------|-----------|-------|
| 10 | **Drinking-water-safe hose** ⚠️ | **NOT a regular garden hose** — standard green hoses aren't potable-rated and leach into your water. Must say "safe for drinking water" / NSF-61. White or blue. Camco TastePURE or Zero-G. | $25–40 |
| 11 | **Water pressure regulator** ⚠️ | RV plumbing wants ~40–50 psi. Well/park pressure can blow lines and fittings. Adjustable w/ gauge is worth the extra few dollars. | $12–35 |
| 12 | Inline carbon water filter | Optional; improves taste, catches sediment. | $25 |
| 13 | 90° hose elbow + washers | Takes the strain off the camper's inlet fitting so it doesn't crack. | $10 |

## Grey Wolf revival (things you'll likely need after 5 years sitting)

| # | Item | Why | ~Cost |
|---|------|-----|-------|
| 14 | **Fresh-water system sanitizer** | 5 years stagnant = biofilm. Bleach works, or Camco TastePURE sanitizer. | $10 |
| 15 | **Water heater anode rod** | Suburban (steel) tanks eat these. After 5 years it's likely gone. (Atwood/aluminum tanks don't use one — check first.) | $15 |
| 16 | **Deep-cycle battery/batteries** ⚠️ | A 5-year-sat RV battery is almost certainly dead. Test before buying, but budget for it. | $120–250 ea |
| 17 | **Propane: refill + recertification** ⚠️ | Tanks need recert every 12 years from date stamp. Check the collar. | $20–60 |
| 18 | **Tires** ⚠️ | **RV tires age out in ~5–7 years REGARDLESS of tread.** Check the DOT date code (last 4 digits = week/year). Sitting causes flat spots + sidewall cracking. Non-negotiable before towing. | $150–250 ea |
| 19 | **Dicor self-leveling lap sealant** | For resealing roof seams. Water intrusion is what kills stored campers. | $12/tube |
| 20 | **CO + LP detectors** ⚠️ | **These EXPIRE (~5–7 yrs).** Check the date stamp — almost certainly due. Life-safety, do not skip. | $25–50 ea |
| 21 | Mouse traps + steel wool | For sealing entry gaps. Assume rodents got in. | $20 |
| 22 | Furnace/AC filter | Cheap, and it's been sitting. | $10 |
